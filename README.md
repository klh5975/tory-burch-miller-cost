## What the $200 leather Miller actually is

Strip out the brand language and the product description is pretty specific. Leather upper, napa leather lining on the underside of the straps, a smooth leather toe post, flexible rubber sole, dust bag included. The Double T is laser-cut leather with hand-painted edges, fastened with gold rivets. The leather comes from a tannery certified by the Leather Working Group, which is a supply-chain standard for leather manufacturing and chemical management rather than a comfort feature.

The one number worth remembering is the footbed: **2.17 inches (55mm) wide at the instep**. Tory Burch calls this "the original fit" and describes it as the narrowest Miller. That detail explains a lot of the fit complaints you'll find in reviews — the shoe people picture when they hear "Miller" is the narrow one.

## Sizing: the official advice and the retailer copy don't agree

Here's a detail most shopping roundups skip. Tory Burch's own product page says the Miller **runs slightly small** and recommends ordering a half size up if you're between sizes. Bloomingdale's, selling the same sandal, says it **fits true to size, order your normal size**.

Same shoe, opposite instructions. I'm not going to pretend one of them is secretly correct — what I'd take from the conflict is that the Miller sits near a size boundary for a lot of people, and that a thong sandal has no laces or buckles to buy you slack. The straps don't adjust on the classic Miller, so a half size wrong is a half size wrong.

What you can act on:

- **Between sizes?** The brand says go up. If you're a solid size with no history of being between sizes, the standard size is the safer first try.
- **Wide feet?** The classic Miller is the narrowest version the brand makes. Go into this expecting the instep measurement above to be the constraint, not the length.
- **Buying to test the fit?** A discounted pair from a legitimate retailer tells you everything a full-price pair would — same last, same shape — and that's usually the smarter way to find out whether the Miller works for your foot.

That's a single review, not a consensus, but it points at the real risk with this purchase: you're paying a premium for a leather flat with a rubber sole, and the value depends on the pair you receive being right.

The honest case for full price: you get the current season's colours, the full size run, the dust bag, and a straightforward return process through the brand. ## Questions that come up before checkout

### Do Tory Burch Miller sandals run small?

The brand says yes — order a half size up if you're between sizes. Bloomingdale's says they fit true to size. Both are describing the same product, so treat the Miller as a shoe to buy with a bit of margin if you're near a boundary. The straps don't adjust.

### Are the patent Miller sandals better than the leather ones?

They cost the same $200. Patent has a glossier finish and stands up better to moisture and stains, which matters if you wear them near water or in unpredictable weather. Plain leather creases and softens with wear, which some people prefer and others don't.

### Do they come with a dust bag?

The classic Miller product page lists a dust bag as included. Don't carry that assumption over to clearance or open-box listings — those come from third parties with their own packaging.

### Where's the cheapest legitimate place to buy?

Based on what's been listed during 2026, the sub-$175 prices have come from clearance channels and department store sale periods rather than the brand's own site. The brand's site is where you go for full size and colour availability. Adding a cashback step on top of whichever you choose is the only saving that applies to both.

### Are the more expensive Miller versions worth it?

The BonBon knotted crystal version is a limited collaboration listed around ¥2,200 CNY at Bloomingdale's — several times the core price. That's a fashion purchase, not a comfort upgrade. The Miller Soft tracks higher than the classic too, and its price history shows it moving $226 to $486 depending on size and date, so it's the version where you should compare listings before committing.
